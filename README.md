Code for “An integrated multi-omics epigenomic atlas of human BMSCs uncovers distinct regulatory architectures governing development and senescence”.

Abstract
===
Bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells (BMSCs) undergo two fundamental functional transitions during their lifespan: development and senescence. However, the epigenomic programs driving these changes remain poorly understood. Here, we constructed an integrated, three-stage multi-omics atlas of human BMSCs from fetal, adult, and elderly donors by generating matched DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression. We show that the silencing of multipotency programs during BMSC development is orchestrated by the cooperative actions of DNA hypermethylation and chromatin compaction at stemness loci. However, senescence is predominantly driven by DNA methylation-dependent gene repression that occurs largely independently of broad chromatin remodeling. Among silenced factors, FOXL1 is epigenetically silenced during BMSC development, and its expression positively correlates with core stemness factors across human tissues, revealing a tissue-spanning stemness-associated expression signature. We further identify RCOR2 as a senescence-linked gene, characterized by progressive hypermethylation and transcriptional silencing during senescence, and its expression is negatively correlated with canonical senescence markers across diverse tissues. Taken together, our findings establish a unified epigenomic framework that distinguishes the developmental and senescent phases of the human BMSC lifecycle and reveal tissue-spanning regulatory principles governing stemness and senescence, and offer actionable insights for preserving cell stemness and advancing regenerative medicine.
